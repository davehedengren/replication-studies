# Replication 179821 — INFEASIBLE

**Paper:** Onuchic & Ray, "Signaling and Discrimination in Collaborative Projects"
**Journal:** *American Economic Review* 113(1), January 2023, 210–252
**Package:** `179821-V1/` (openICPSR 179821)

## Why this paper is out of scope

This is a pure game-theoretic paper with **no external data and no empirical
estimation**. The paper builds a model of pairwise collaboration under
reputational concerns, characterizes equilibrium collaboration sets via
Proposition 1, and studies the fragility of symmetric equilibria
(Propositions 2–8, Corollaries 1–3) when the public assigns credit to a
payoff-irrelevant identity. All "results" in the paper are theorems,
propositions, and corollaries proved in the body and appendix. No dataset
is analyzed — the authors' own Data Availability statement in `README.pdf`
says so explicitly:

> This paper does not involve analysis of external data (the only data are
> generated by the authors via simulation in their code).

The shipped replication package contains **three** files total:

1. `Code for Figures 1 and 2.tex` — tikz/pgfplots source that draws the two
   schematic figures illustrating equilibrium collaboration regions as
   subsets of the locus `{(x, y) : f(x, y) = z}`. Nothing is computed;
   the coordinates are hard-coded in LaTeX.
2. `Code for Figure 3.ipynb` — a single Python/scipy Jupyter notebook that
   simulates the equilibrium correspondence under an exponential idea
   distribution (`g(w, 1) = λ₁ e^(-λ₁ w)`, `g(w, 0) = λ₀ e^(-λ₀ w)`, with
   `λ₀ = 4, λ₁ = 1, p = q = 0.5, α = 0.1`) and plots two curves — the
   probability of collaboration and the "size" of the collaboration set —
   in both the symmetric and asymmetric equilibria as functions of the
   joint output `z ∈ [1.6, 5.0]`.
3. `README.pdf` — computational-environment notes.

There are no regression tables, no standard errors, no observational
sample sizes, no parameter estimates, no identification strategy, no data
audit possible, and no robustness tests to run. This fits the driver's
**"MATLAB-only / pure-theory structural model with no empirical component"**
infeasibility criterion explicitly. The closest analogues in the repo are
`replication_130626` (Acemoglu et al., multi-group SIR optimal control),
`replication_141001` (Hatchondo et al., sovereign-default structural model),
and `replication_147524` (Bouakez et al., multi-sector DSGE), all also
skipped for the same reason.

## What we did do — Figure 3 sanity check

For completeness, the notebook was ported to a standalone script
(`figure3.py`) and run under the shared venv. It reproduces both panels of
the published Figure 3 to visual identity, at the parameter values stated
in the figure caption (`p = q = 0.5, α = 0.1, g(w, 1) = e^(-w),
g(w, 0) = 4 e^(-4w)`). Numerical values at a few grid points:

| quantity                            | z = 1.60 | z = 3.28 | z = 5.00 |
|-------------------------------------|---------:|---------:|---------:|
| Prob collab — symmetric equilibrium |  0.11613 |  0.37374 |  0.56952 |
| Prob collab — asymmetric equilibrium|  0.12199 |  0.28738 |  0.29166 |
| Set size — symmetric equilibrium    |  0.27548 |  1.92271 |  3.87387 |
| Set size — asymmetric equilibrium   |  0.24665 |  0.62755 |  1.02102 |

The two curves in the left panel cross at roughly `z ≈ 2.8`, exactly
matching the published Figure 3 (p. 230). The symmetric-equilibrium
collaboration-set size strictly dominates the asymmetric one on the entire
plotted range, consistent with Proposition 8 / Corollary 3. This confirms
that the shipped simulation code runs under modern Python/numpy/scipy and
that the illustrative claims in Section V.A (aggregate welfare in the
exponential model) are internally consistent with what the code produces.

But **this is not a replication of an empirical finding.** It is a
re-run of an author-produced numerical simulation of an author-specified
parametric example, used in the paper purely as an illustration of
Propositions 1 and 8 in one specific tractable sub-case. The economic
content of the paper — fragility, asymmetric equilibrium, discrimination
implications — is established by proof, not by this plot. There is
nothing to audit, no published coefficient to compare against, and no way
to stress-test the theorems through data.

## What would be required for a "real" replication

A meaningful replication of 179821 would require either

- **a formal-methods pass** (Coq/Lean/Isabelle) verifying Propositions
  1–8 and Corollaries 1–3, which is outside the scope of this repo and
  several orders of magnitude larger than any of the empirical papers
  it contains; or
- **an empirical companion study** — e.g., testing the paper's
  prediction that credit allocation in coauthored work is biased along
  payoff-irrelevant identity lines, using the kind of data referenced
  in the paper's footnotes (Sarsons et al. 2021 tenure/coauthorship,
  Ductor–Goyal–Prummer 2021 coauthorship networks, Lissoni et al. 2013
  patents) — which is an independent research project, not a replication.

Neither is in scope for this automated driver.
